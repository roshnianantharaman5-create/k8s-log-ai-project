A plain-English field guide

From a noisy log to a helpful answer.

This project watches a small Kubernetes app, collects what happens, and uses a machine-learning model to answer one practical question: “Does anything look like trouble right now?”

01 · The story

Imagine a little town with a very good lookout.

The demo app is the town. Kubernetes is the town planner. Logs are the notes people leave behind. The observability stack gathers those notes, and the prediction service reads them for patterns.

The app

Game 2048 is our busy shop

It gives us something real to visit in a browser. It runs inside Kubernetes, just like a production service would.

The lookout

The log generator makes practice signals

It creates normal events and failure-like events—timeouts, errors, slow requests, and memory pressure—so the demo is never silent.

The guide

The AI gives a risk estimate

The model turns recent logs into a simple result: normal, watch closely, or likely failing.

03 · The signals

What does “observability” mean here?

It means asking the system questions and getting clues back. The project collects three kinds of clues:

✓
Logs · what happened?Messages such as “request timed out” or “service started.”
✓
Metrics · how much?Counts and measurements such as requests, errors, and memory use.
✓
Traces · where did time go?A journey map showing which part of a request was slow.
04 · The brain

How the prediction works.

We train the model on labelled examples. In everyday language: we show it many “normal” and “problem” notes, then ask it to judge a fresh batch of notes.

Learn

Turn notes into signals

Words become useful features, while numbers such as latency and error rate are scaled into a common range.

Decide

Estimate the chance of trouble

The classifier produces a probability. It is a warning light, not a replacement for human judgment.

Explain

Show the evidence

The API returns the probability, risk level, number of records, and the types of events it saw.
